# Humanized: Wisdom Curve article

## Final version

The design industry pays for speed and ignores synthesis.

We celebrate the 25-year-old who ships fast. We quietly push out the 50-year-old who sees patterns the 25-year-old won't recognize for another two decades.

Darren Yeo wrote about this in "The Wisdom Curve," using the framework of crystallized vs. fluid intelligence. Fluid intelligence peaks in your 30s. Crystallized intelligence is still climbing in your 60s. The piece is worth reading, but I think it undersells what's actually happening right now.

Yesterday I built a knowledge system architecture. Not in a sprint, not writing requirements. I sat with an AI and started pulling threads through Burroughs, Cornell shadow boxes, Murakami, and a half-remembered Alastair Reynolds novel. I couldn't have told you in advance what the architecture would look like. Twenty years of reading across disciplines gave me the raw material. The AI let me externalize it fast enough that I could actually see the connections before I lost them. That's the part that's new. I've had those ideas before. I just couldn't get them out at the speed of thought until now.

That's not replacing a team. It's closer to finally having a long enough table to lay out all the pieces at once.

Here's what I keep coming back to: we over-index on toolsets because tools are easy to sell. But anyone who's survived three platform shifts knows the tool is temporary. What you learned to see along the way isn't. And when that kind of deep pattern recognition meets a tool that can actually keep up with it, you don't get automation. You get something that didn't exist before.

I don't have a clean word for it. "Actualization" is the closest I've found, but it sounds like a self-help book. Whatever you call it, it's the thing where knowledge that's been locked in your head for decades finally gets to have a body.

The industry's obsession with speed is going to age badly. The people with twenty years of cross-domain pattern recognition are just getting started.
